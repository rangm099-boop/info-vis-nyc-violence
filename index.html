Gun violence, seen more clearly

What do the numbers say about our city?

Gun violence is often discussed through headlines. Looking at the data can help us ask better questions about what is happening, where, and when.

01 / Context

One city. Many everyday experiences.

Gun violence is not only a statistic. It can shape how people think about their neighbourhoods, daily routes, work, school and public spaces.

This project asks how location-specific shooting data can help New Yorkers compare what they assume about safety with what public records actually show.

04 / The turn

A citywide decline is important. It does not tell the whole neighbourhood story.

These figures describe New York City as a whole. They cannot tell us, by themselves, how risk differs block by block, what causes an incident, or how every resident experiences safety.

The next question is more local: how do patterns change by borough, precinct, location and time? That is why the campaign points readers toward the underlying public datasets rather than treating one headline as the whole story.

01 / LOOK CLOSER

Move from a citywide total to the location and time fields in the public data.

02 / ASK QUESTIONS

Compare patterns carefully and consider what the dataset does not measure.

03 / KEEP CONTEXT

Use official records as evidence, not as a complete explanation of community safety.

05 / The ask

Look beyond the headline.

Explore the records. Question the patterns. Understand the limits. A clearer picture begins with better questions.
